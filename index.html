THE PAPER

Abstract

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) has emerged as a promising paradigm for safety alignment, delivering dense, token-level supervision by distilling from a teacher conditioned on refusal-oriented privileged prompts. However, we reveal that this paradigm suffers from critical inefficiencies that degrade both training efficiency and general reasoning capabilities. Specifically, we diagnose two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) supervisory collapse over extended rollouts, where the teacher’s corrective efficacy degrades precipitously as the student’s generation prefix lengthens, injecting noisy gradients into late-stage tokens; and (2) gradient dilution from stylistic shifts, where the distillation objective is dominated by safety-irrelevant stylistic discrepancies induced by privileged prompting, washing out genuine safety signals and impairing base reasoning.

To resolve these issues, we propose Efficient On-Policy Self-Distilled Safety Alignment (EOPSA), which concentrates computational and gradient budgets exclusively on reliably supervised, safety-critical tokens. EOPSA incorporates two coordinated mechanisms: Adaptive Rollout Scheduling, which dynamically bounds the generation horizon guided by Teacher Rescue Rate (TRR), and Selective Distillation, which filters out safety-neutral tokens to restrict gradient updates to safety-pivotal transitions. Extensive evaluations across reasoning models up to 32B parameters demonstrate that EOPSA slashes rollout computation by ~50% and backpropagates through merely ~2% of tokens, consistently outperforming full-token distillation baselines in both safety compliance and reasoning retention.

01 / SUPERVISORY RELIABILITY

Longer prefixes become harder to rescue.

TRR measures teacher continuation on student prefixes. IDR reverses the roles, measuring student continuation on safe teacher prefixes.

02 / SEMANTIC RELEVANCE

High divergence can reflect style.

The high-KL subset contains many function words, identical top-1 predictions, and safety-neutral lexical shifts.

METHOD

Adaptive horizons. Selective gradients.

EOPSA coordinates sequence-level truncation and token-level semantic filtering.

01

Adaptive Rollout Scheduling

Select the longest candidate horizon whose validation Teacher Rescue Rate meets the reliability threshold. The default candidates are {128, 256, 1024}, with τ = 0.75.

L = max { l ∈ S : TRRl ≥ τ }

Selective Distillation

Use the top-1 student–teacher token pair to classify each position. Retain Pivot, Intent, and Risk; filter Function, Consistent, and Other. Match teacher and student distributions only at selected positions.
